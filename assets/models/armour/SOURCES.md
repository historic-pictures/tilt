# Armour, rider and lance: reference sources

The models in this folder are our own work (parametric Blender scripts in `tools/blender/`); nothing here is a scan or a copy of a mesh.
Their proportions were traced from the photographs below. The photographs themselves are not shipped: they are kept, git-ignored, in
`shots/armour2/ref/` (re-download from the linked Wikimedia Commons pages). Only numbers measured from them are in the repository
(`tools/blender/refs/*.json`).

Licences: CC0 / public domain need no credit; the CC BY and CC BY-SA photographs are credited here. We publish no derivative of the
CC BY-SA photographs (measurements and silhouettes only), so their share-alike term does not reach the game's assets.

## Other data
- **ANSUR II** (U.S. Army anthropometric survey, 2012, public domain): male means scaled to 1.73 m for the side (sagittal) depths of
  the trunk, legs and arms. No free strict-profile photograph of a standing man was found on Commons, so the side view of the bare
  body is checked against these depths, not against a photograph.

## How they were used
1. `tools/blender/trace_body.py` reads the silhouette of the front photograph of the man, scales it crown-to-sole to the rig (1.73 m), maps
   heights piecewise onto the rig's joints and writes `refs/body_front.json`.
2. `tools/blender/human.py` builds the trunk and limbs from that trace; every plate is that surface at a stand-off (`torso_shape.py`,
   `riderlib.py`).
3. `refs/harness_milanese.json` and `refs/harness_bascinet.json` hold landmark breadths read from the two full-figure museum photographs
   (`trace_photo.py` for the outline), scaled sole-to-shoulder to the rig.
4. `tools/blender/check_silhouette.py` skins the game glb into the references' pose, measures its front and side silhouettes at the same
   landmarks and fails over a threshold: `python check_silhouette.py body`, `python check_silhouette.py harness milanese|bascinet`.
5. `tools/blender/overlay.py` writes the overlay proofs in `shots/armour2/`.
